# Proof and conversion plan

Last updated: 26 August 2026 (Europe/Athens)

## Positioning decision

A4T leads as a founder-led design studio that gets work finished. The primary offer is brand, website, and app delivery. Photography is the strongest accessible entry service and a source of immediate independent proof. Commercial direction supports those delivery projects. Specialist writing is available through a named partner when the brief needs it. Responsible-growth advice is not promoted as a standalone offer until there is a qualified specialist and evidence.

The former free app-idea call, app-onboarding offer, and six-month app partnership are removed from the enquiry form. They should return only as one clearly scoped paid discovery offer with a price, deliverables, eligibility, and proof.

## First three arms-length references

1. **Business photography starter:** Sell a fixed-scope portrait or small-business shoot from £250 to a customer with no family or partner relationship. Agree the brief, edited image count, usage, delivery date, and permission to describe the factual project before work begins.
2. **Website clarity sprint:** Sell a small independent business a fixed first stage covering content structure, one design direction, and a working responsive site or prototype. Set the final public price after timing and technical scope are confirmed; do not advertise an unsupported figure.
3. **Brand launch package:** Deliver a contained identity-and-launch project for an independent organisation: positioning, essential identity files, core copy, and a practical launch set. Keep social management and production extras outside the initial scope unless separately priced.

Use fixed introductory scopes before broad discounts. A limited discount can reflect a smaller scope or permission to publish a factual case study, but it must never be conditional on a positive review. Do not present owned ventures, family-connected work, or partner work as arms-length client validation.

## Testimonial publication bar

Do not link the public website to `/reviews/` until all of the following are true:

- At least three testimonials are verified and approved for publication.
- They represent at least two arms-length customers.
- Each names the service or deliverable and says something specific about the work or result.
- The author name, role or business, relationship, and publication consent are recorded.
- Any edits are limited to spelling or length and approved by the author.

Once the bar is met, publish a compact evidence component beneath the homepage proof section and on the relevant service page. Each item should contain a short quotation, person, role or company, service label, date or project period, and a link to the related case study where available. Avoid star scores until there is enough comparable review volume to make them meaningful.

## Photography review request

> Hi [Name], I hope you are well. I am improving the way I present my photography work and wondered if you would be comfortable sharing a short, honest note about the shoot we worked on. It would be especially helpful to know what I photographed for you, how the process felt, and whether the finished images were useful. There is no need to make it promotional, and I will only publish it with your permission. Here is the private form: https://askfortask.co.uk/reviews/

Send this only to people with a genuine past working relationship. Record the invitation and do not follow up more than once unless they reply.

## DMAR disclosure

Keep a concise relationship label near the first appearance of DMAR and the fuller “who did what” explanation inside the case study. The consultancy is led by Nikita Piazenko's father; A4T delivered the identity, commercial structure, website, and launch routes. Moving that fact into small print would reduce trust rather than improve it.

## Rollout order

1. Publish the narrowed positioning, starter photography price, shorter enquiry form, and consistent reply promise.
2. Sell and complete the first independent fixed-scope project.
3. Request specific, consented feedback after delivery.
4. Add testimonials only after the publication bar is met.
5. Turn the first three independent projects into concise case studies with scope, role, timing, evidence, and result.

## Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Broad capability list weakens credibility | Lead with studio delivery; keep supporting capabilities subordinate |
| Discount attracts unsuitable work | Prefer fixed scope; state exclusions and revision limits before quoting |
| Review request feels pressured | Ask for honest feedback, not a positive review; publication is optional |
| Connected work looks like independent proof | Label owned, family-connected, and partner relationships wherever shown |
| Fast-response promise becomes absolute | Promise acknowledgement by the next business day; allow longer for detailed scope |
